# Music and BGM Plan

Background music decides whether the audience *feels* a scene. This plan makes sure **every situation has the right BGM**, that the main themes are **catchy and memorable** (people should hum them after watching), and that the music stays consistent across the whole series.

Tool: **Suno Pro** (needed for commercial use), **or Gemini's own music tool (Lyria) in the Gemini app** if you're following the [Gemini-only workflow](GEMINI-ONLY-WORKFLOW.md). The same prompts work in both. Prompts below go in Suno's **Style** box; turn **Instrumental ON** unless lyrics are given. Don't use real composer or film names in prompts; Suno blocks them, and your channel should have its own sound anyway.

---

## 1. How the music works in this series

### 1.1 Leitmotifs: one catchy theme per character and feeling
Like a film score, each important character or feeling has its **own short melody (a "hook" of 4–8 notes)**. The audience learns them, and every time a theme returns they feel something before anything happens on screen.

| Theme | Plays when | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 Series title theme** (with vocal hook "கண்ணா... கண்ணா...") | Opening titles, end card, and the biggest emotional payoffs | The series' identity; the most catchy |
| **T2 Kannan's theme** | Kannan is being mischievous, clever, playful | Bouncy, cheeky flute |
| **T3 Yashoda's theme (Mother's love)** | Hugs, forgiveness, tenderness | Warm veena + strings |
| **T4 Butter-gang theme** | The friends together, plans, running | Upbeat folk groove; whistle-able |
| **T5 Heist / sneak theme** | Tiptoeing, stealing, hiding | Pizzicato + ghatam, comic suspense |
| **T6 Chase theme** | Running away, being chased | Fast thavil + nadaswaram; high energy |
| **T7 Gokulam theme** | Village life, dawn, establishing shots | Peaceful pastoral flute |
| **T8 Divine theme** | Miracles, the universe, Kannan's true nature | Choir hum, temple bells, rising strings |
| **T9 Mystery motif** | Hints of divinity (eating mud, his calm eyes) | One bell + drone |
| **T10 Kamsa's theme** | Mathura, Kamsa, danger | Dark drums, low brass, heavy |
| **T11 Lullaby (sung)** | Night, sleep, the end of an episode | Tender Tamil lullaby |

**Variations:** keep the *same melody* and change the *arrangement* for different moods. Kannan's theme can be playful (flute), sad (slow nadaswaram) or grand (orchestra and choir). In Suno, use **Cover** (re-style a song you've made while keeping its melody) or **Extend** to create these versions from the original.

### 1.2 What makes a cue "catchy"
- A **clear melody in the first 5 seconds**, simple enough to whistle.
- **Repetition:** the hook repeats at least twice in a cue.
- A **strong rhythm** for fun, action and chase scenes (mridangam, thavil, kanjira, claps).
- **Contrast:** catchy themes hit harder when they follow silence or a soft passage.
- When choosing between Suno generations, **pick the one you're still humming 5 minutes later**.

### 1.3 Musical colour (ragas as flavour)
Suno doesn't follow ragas strictly, but naming a raga steers the colour in the right direction. Use these as flavour words:

| Mood | Raga flavour |
|---|---|
| Joy, play, village morning | Mohanam, Bilahari |
| Love, tenderness, romance | Kalyani, Kapi, Behag |
| Devotion, the divine | Sindhu Bhairavi, Kalyani, Revati |
| Mystery | Hindolam |
| Sadness, separation | Shivaranjani, Mukhari |
| Valour, battle | Atana, Arabhi |
| Celebration | Mohanam, Kummi folk rhythm |

### 1.4 Where there should be NO music
Silence is part of the score. **Cut the music completely** for:
- the first 2–3 seconds of the cold open (whisper + ambience grabs attention better);
- the moment just before a big reveal or shock (the kolusu sound, "வாயைத் திற", the cut to black);
- any moment where a sound effect *is* the drama (the anklets, the door bursting open).

## 6. Putting the music into the edit (CapCut)

1. **Lay the music after the animatic and dialogue are locked** ([04 §B1](04-voices-and-cinematic-flow.md#b1-work-in-this-order-the-professional-secret-the-animatic)).
2. **Start and end cues on purpose:** begin on a cut or an action; end on a "button" (a final note) or a hard stop, never by fading out randomly.
3. **Hit points:** move the cue so its accents land on actions (pot crash, door burst, wink, the reveal). If the cue doesn't fit, cut the music at a bar line or use a stinger.
4. **Duck under dialogue:** music drops to −24/−26 dB while anyone speaks and rises between lines (CapCut keyframes or auto-ducking). With **stems**, lower only the melody under speech and keep the drums.
5. **Crossfade between cues** that are in a similar mood (0.5–1 s). Use a **hard cut or silence** between contrasting moods (the comedy → divine change).
6. **Ambience never stops:** even when the music stops, the scene's ambience bed continues.
7. **Final balance:** dialogue −6 dB peak · music Under −24 to −26 dB / Mid −18 / Full −12 to −14 · SFX −12 to −18 · overall −14 LUFS.

## 7. BGM checklist (add to QC)
- [ ] Every scene has a planned cue (or a deliberate silence) from the cue sheet
- [ ] The mood of the music matches the situation (comedy ≠ divine ≠ danger)
- [ ] Themes return for the same character or feeling (T2 for Kannan, T3 for Yashoda, T10 for Kamsa)
- [ ] Action, chase and fun cues have a strong rhythm and a catchy hook
- [ ] Music never covers a line of dialogue
- [ ] Hits land exactly on the actions
- [ ] Silences are in place before the big moments
- [ ] No sudden random fade-outs; every cue starts and ends on purpose
- [ ] All music is from your own Suno Pro account (commercial rights) or properly licensed
